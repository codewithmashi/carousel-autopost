Your AI agent can be given orders by a web page. And it has no reliable way to tell those orders apart from yours.

That's prompt injection — and it isn't a bug someone forgot to patch. It's structural. Your instructions and the data your agent reads arrive in the same stream of text. Nothing in that stream marks where "you" end and "the internet" begins.

Which is why hidden text in a document, a product review, or an email signature can say "ignore prior instructions" — and to the model, that's not an attack. It's just more text.

Filters help. They don't close it. You can't blocklist all of language.

The part that changed in 2026: a chatbot with this flaw gives a bad answer. An agent with this flaw takes a bad action — with your inbox, your files, your repos, your card.

What actually helps:
→ Least privilege. Scope every tool tightly.
→ A human confirms anything irreversible.
→ Treat fetched content as data, never as commands.
